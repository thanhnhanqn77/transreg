Identify semantic correspondences across premises and the question.

In particular:

- Detect different surface expressions that denote the same predicate or semantic relation.
- Align paraphrases when they express the same meaning with compatible argument roles.
  Examples:
    "AMC List" / "AMC service"
    "prefer TV series more" / "prefer TV series to movies"
- Prefer one canonical meaning for equivalent expressions instead of creating separate meanings only because wording differs.
- Do NOT merge expressions merely because they are related. If meanings differ, record them in must_remain_distinct.
- Record textual evidence for every important alignment.

Also identify logical scope and restriction patterns that later translation must preserve.

For relative-clause constructions such as:

    "people/customers who P ... Q"

treat P as a restriction/condition on the subject set and Q as the main consequence.
Record this relation in scope_constraints.

For example:

    "Customers who prefer TV series will not watch TV series in cinemas"

should be analyzed as:

    restriction: prefer TV series
    consequence: not watch TV series in cinemas
    relation: conditional

Do NOT interpret the restriction and consequence as two properties asserted universally of every entity.

For:
    "not a person P and a Q"

first group the coordinated phrase as (P and Q), then negate the entire group:
    not (P and Q)

Do not interpret it as:
    (not P) and Q

Apply the same scope discipline inside implication antecedents.

Under the shared FOLIO representation policy:

- Generic human-domain expressions such as "people", "persons", and similar domain nouns should not automatically become independent type predicates or guards when they merely describe the discourse domain.
- Likewise, domain nouns such as "customers" may function as discourse-domain wording when the problem consistently reasons about customers.
- Do not infer an explicit category fact such as Customer(James) unless it is stated.
- Instead, record the domain interpretation in context so the translator follows one consistent representation policy.

Identify recurring individuals and coreference across sentences.

Identify equivalent personal-work wording such as published/authored/wrote when the shared FOLIO convention supports a common compositional interpretation, while keeping publishers/companies and authors distinct.

Keep semantically different relations distinct even when they are closely related.

The question is a query, not an additional premise.

The global analysis and accepted registry cannot override these scope and semantic-alignment rules.

described in its question can be a designated discourse individual. Distinguish
it from explicit some/all and from a newly quantified individual. Stable
coreference does not assert membership merely from a constant's spelling.
