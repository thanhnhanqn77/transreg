CANONICAL FOLIO INTERPRETATION POLICY (shared by translation and verification)
Judge the most natural, dataset-consistent reading under these explicit rules.
Ambiguity alone is NOT a semantic error. Do not reject a candidate solely because
an alternative English reading is theoretically possible. Require a concrete
mismatch with the source under this policy. Do not use gold answers or invent facts.

- Do not automatically introduce a type predicate for generic domain nouns such as
  "people", "customers", "persons", etc. if the sentence is naturally quantified
  over that discourse class (e.g. "Customers either subscribe to AMC service or HBO service" ->
  forall x (SubscribedToAMC(x) xor SubscribedToHBO(x))). Keep substantive restrictions
  (Doctor, Student, Bird, etc.). In generic statements the quantification domain is that
  discourse class; the solver's sort label Entity does not broaden that domain or demand
  an extra Person or Customer guard. A back-translation's "every entity" denotes this same
  discourse domain. Rejecting solely for missing Person/Customer contradicts this explicit policy.
- Prefer canonical reusable semantic predicates over surface-form predicates.
- When a meaning can be compositionally represented using existing predicates,
  reuse/decompose into those predicates instead of introducing a new monolithic predicate.
- Preserve the representation style used consistently across the current problem and the few-shot examples.
- Do not add facts that are not stated or logically licensed.
- Evaluate semantic correctness under the same FOLIO representation conventions
  used by the translator and few-shot demonstrations. Do not reject a candidate translation
  merely because another logically possible formalization exists (such as demanding a redundant
  x != y distinctness guard when none was asserted, or rejecting an established relational phrasing).
- Plain "or" uses inclusive disjunction canonically: (expr1 or expr2).
  "either ... or ..." uses exclusive disjunction canonically: (expr1 xor expr2).
  In FOLIO dataset structure and SymbCoT translation conventions, natural language
  "either A or B" denotes logical exclusive disjunction (expr1 ⊕ expr2 / xor), whereas
  plain "A or B" denotes inclusive disjunction (expr1 ∨ expr2 / or).
  Thus "People either regularly drink coffee or joke about caffeine" translates
  canonically to forall x (RegularlyDrinkCoffee(x) xor JokesAboutCaffeine(x)).
- Every/all/each A is B: forall x (A(x) -> B(x)). Explicit some/there exists:
  exists x (A(x) and B(x)). The article a/an ALONE does not select exists:
  a generic class rule ("A patient with a fever needs rest") is universal;
  an episodic introduction can be existential; a recurring definite/anaphoric
  subject preserves its designated referent. Decide from the whole source,
  including the query. Never generalize a designated "his adviser" into all
  advisers or identify a category with a newly invented individual constant.
  No A is B: forall x (A(x) -> not B(x)). Statements with relative
  clauses like "Xs who P do Q", "People who P do Q", or "Customers who P and will not Q"
  express universal conditional rules: forall x (P(x) -> Q(x)). Do NOT reject this
  canonical universal implication or demand a universal conjunction forall x (P(x) and Q(x)).
  Preserve explicit existential assertions, universal restrictions and quantifier scope;
  do not instantiate unspecified individuals as named constants or add existential import.
- Grammatical past/present tense alone is neutral in timeless FOLIO properties.
  Preserve explicitly asserted temporal relations or dates; tense neutrality does
  not license removing these facts.
- If A then B is A -> B; A only if B is A -> B. Do not reverse the implication,
  strengthen it to equivalence, omit its condition, or add unsupported conditions.
- Preserve negation and coordination scope. "Neither A nor B" means not A and
  not B. "Not a P and a Q" negates the coordinated noun phrase; "not P and is Q"
  negates only P. Preserve lexical unaware as a property; do not silently equate
  it with a different predicate or add an unasserted relationship.
- Preserve exact cardinality, at-least/at-most bounds, distinct witnesses and
  equality/inequality; no unique-name or closed-world assumptions by default.
- Reject actual wrong predicates, entities, argument roles/order, quantifiers,
  implication direction, missing negation or clauses, and unsupported additions.
  For example "Every doctor is a researcher" is NOT equivalent to
  forall x (Researcher(x) -> Doctor(x)). This is a concrete reversed implication.

FOLIO CONTEXT AND REGISTRY ALIGNMENT (apply to premises and question alike)
- For a personal biographical statement "NAME published a book in YEAR called
  TITLE", use the benchmark personal-work convention: Book(TITLE),
  Author(NAME,TITLE), PublishedIn(TITLE,YEAR). A query that someone "wrote a book
  in YEAR" uses these SAME relations with existential variables, plus any stated
  restriction such as Czech(person). Keep the year and named title. This is a
  narrow FOLIO personal-work convention, NOT a universal publisher=author rule.
  Do not apply it to publishing companies, editors, translators, or publication
  explicitly attributed to someone else's authorship. New unrelated meanings
  still need distinct predicates. A generic query must not be instantiated as
  the known named author just to make a proof succeed.
- Determine whether a singular noun phrase is a designated discourse individual
  or a quantified category using the whole problem, and preserve that choice
  through the question. In FOLIO's categorical puzzle about "a sea eel" followed
  by "Sea eel is ...", the recurring sea eel is the SAME designated individual:
  use SeaEel as a constant in the conditional and question. Do not translate the
  premise as a rule guarded by SeaEel(x), then ask Paper(SeaEel) about an unrelated
  constant. Do not assert Eel(SeaEel) merely because its name contains "eel".
  Explicit all/some sea eels remain quantified; this is not a rule making every
  common noun a constant. Surface-name identity is not an extra taxonomy fact.
- An asserted naming identity ("X is another name for Y") is equality between
  stable constants, not a unary property or an unconnected new predicate.
  Keep construction dates distinct from register-entry dates and preserve
  location, nationality, roles, named works, numeric counts and other clauses.
- Negated coordinated noun phrases have joint scope: "not a person dependent
  on caffeine and a student" is not (Dependent(x) and Student(x)), whereas
  "not dependent and is a student" is (not Dependent(x)) and Student(x).
- FOL identifiers cannot contain hyphens, spaces or apostrophes. Use L2021 for
  the named monitor "L-2021"; retain the original surface spelling in its meaning.
  L-2021 inside FOL is subtraction, not an entity name. Reuse canonical spelling
  across every premise and question. Do not change true numeric expressions.
